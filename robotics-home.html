Featured · Humanoid robot learning

Teaching a Unitree G1 humanoid to move from ordinary video

GVHMR2PBHC is a pipeline that turns a phone video of a person into motion a humanoid robot can learn. A reinforcement-learning policy learns to track that motion in Isaac Gym. It's checked in simulation, then deployed on the physical robot.

Pipeline

  1. Video30 fps clip of a person
  2. PoseGVHMR to SMPL body model
  3. Convert.pt to .npz, with checks
  4. RetargetMink IK onto the G1's 29 joints
  5. StabilizeSelective locking and smoothing
  6. TrainRL motion tracking, Isaac Gym, 4,096 envs
  7. DeployPhysical Unitree G1

What I built

  • Selective motion locking. Full-body motion made RL training unstable, so the tool freezes chosen joint groups (legs, root, upper body: six presets and an adjustable split) and keeps the expressive part of the motion. It can even hold poses no human could, like a 90° waist twist with still legs.
  • A working retargeting stage. The stock script's output had swapped Y and Z axes and the wrong parameter types, so it couldn't feed training. My version writes training-ready motion.
  • The glue between research tools. Converters and checkers for every hand-off (.pt, .npz, .pkl) and a motion inspector, so each stage's output can be verified before the next.
  • Smoothing and transitions. Interpolates the motion and adds an intro transition before training.

Assistive robotics · Hardware

Tremor-filtering active ball joint

A shoulder-like robotic joint to help people with hand tremors: it senses motion, filters out the tremor and drives smooth movement.

  • Built around an ABENICS-style spherical-gear ball joint, modelled in Fusion 360 and 3D printed.
  • Accelerometer and flex-sensor input processed on an Arduino Uno, driving stepper motors.
  • The prototype reproduced shoulder-like motion and filtered tremor-induced movement.

Reinforcement learning · C

Self-play Q-learning bot for Chinese Checkers

A simplified Chinese Checkers game and a bot that learns it by playing itself, written in C, with Human vs Human and Human vs Bot modes.

  • Stage 1: two bots build Q-tables by playing each other.
  • Stage 2: a challenger has to beat the reigning best bot over a 100-game streak to replace it.
  • Stage 3: reward weights are re-tuned and a tournament picks the final bot.
