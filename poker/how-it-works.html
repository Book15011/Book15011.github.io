Game theory · Imperfect information

How the poker AI works

In chess you can see the whole board. In poker you can't, so the right move depends on what your opponent might hold, and that depends on what they think you might hold. This project is a working attempt at that problem: a heads-up No-Limit Hold'em AI, trained offline and solving live in your browser.

Play against it ♠

The approach in four steps

OfflineGroup similar hands

K-means clustering reduces billions of situations to a few thousand buckets per street.

OfflineLearn by self-play

Monte Carlo counterfactual regret minimization plays billions of hands against itself, moving toward a strategy that is hard to exploit.

OfflineShip only what's needed

Only preflop and flop strategies are exported, as compact tables. No server is needed after this.

In your browserSolve the rest live

On the turn and river, a WebAssembly solver in a background thread works out the rest of the hand, including the exact size you bet.

This is the recipe from Modicum and Pluribus, at a far smaller scale. I also looked at Deep CFR and DeepStack-style value networks. Value networks were out of reach (DeepStack's turn network alone took over 175 core-years of solving), and a neural policy had no evidence of beating this design on the hardware available. The research notes cover the comparison.

What runs where

PartWhenWhat it does
Card bucketsOfflineClusters hands by strength and how they may develop, on every board
BlueprintOfflineAverage strategy from self-play, one table per stack depth
Range trackingLiveUpdates what each player could hold, from public betting only
Preflop and flopLiveLooks up the trained strategy, translating unusual bet sizes
Turn and riverLiveSolves the rest of the hand with Discounted CFR, about a second or two, and keeps solving while you decide

The AI runs in a Web Worker that only ever receives its own view of the table: its two cards, the board and the betting. It has no route to your cards or the deck. There is no language model and no paid API, and nothing leaves your browser.

Benchmark results

All results run the same JavaScript and WebAssembly that ship to your browser. Hand-written opponents play duplicate deals (each deck twice, with seats swapped) to cancel card luck. Ranges are 95% confidence intervals. Win rates are in big blinds per 100 hands.

What this doesn't show

  • It is not a solution of poker. The blueprint is an equilibrium of a simplified game, and the solver uses estimated ranges and a limited set of bet sizes.
  • It has not played professionals. Beating bots, including strong ones, is not the same as beating people who can adapt over thousands of hands.
  • It does not adapt to you. It aims to be hard to exploit, rather than to punish your specific habits, so it can win less from weak players than a good human would.
  • One quick match is mostly luck. A win or loss over 15 hands says little either way.
